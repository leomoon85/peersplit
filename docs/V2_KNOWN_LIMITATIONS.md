# PeerSplit V2 Known Limitations & Future Roadmap

## 1. Current Architectural Constraints

1. **Maximum Parents Per Operation:**
   - The protocol envelope constrains `parents` to a maximum of 32 IDs (`ProtocolVersion.MAX_PARENTS = 32`).
   - In extremely large, concurrent peer networks where more than 32 concurrent branches exist simultaneously, DAG heads are truncated to 32 lexicographical items. Unreferenced heads will be merged in subsequent causal operations.

2. **Single Currency Per Group:**
   - A single PeerSplit group operates strictly in its designated currency (e.g. INR, USD, EUR).
   - Multi-currency transactions within a single group are rejected with `CURRENCY_MISMATCH`. Users tracking multi-currency expenses must create dedicated groups per currency.

3. **Nostr Relay Persistence Dependency:**
   - While the local device state is fully durable in SQLite Room v7, inter-device synchronization relies on public or private Nostr relays.
   - The local durable outbox (`outbox_operations`) supports queued delivery after reconnection. Relay pruning, unavailable peers and lost device copies can still prevent historical recovery; reconnection alone does not guarantee that every peer receives all history.
   - Do not rely on public relays as a permanent archive. Keep password-encrypted backups under your control; a privately managed relay also requires its own retention, security and recovery arrangements.

4. **Offline Member ("Ghost Member") Key Autonomy:**
   - Non-app members ("Ghost Members") have deterministic public keys generated by the group creator.
   - When an offline member joins the app, they claim the ghost identity via a `MemberAdded` operation referencing `claimedGhostPubkey`. Historical operations authored prior to the claim retain the original ghost identifier and are resolved at reconciliation time via `ghostAliases`.

---

## 2. Future Protocol Roadmap (V2.1+)

- **Advanced Group Key Management:** Full MLS-style (Messaging Layer Security) tree-key ratcheting for groups with high membership churn, beyond the existing group-key epoch rotation. Rotation cannot revoke historical keys or records already copied by a member.
- **Pruning & Snapshotting:** Creating periodic verifiable state snapshots (e.g. at 1,000 operations) to allow lightweight clients to sync without replaying full history.
- **Threshold Signatures (FROST):** Multi-party threshold signature support for group administration and group closure.
